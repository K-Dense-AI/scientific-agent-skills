# Medical Structured Abstract Drafting Examples

**Reviewed: 2026-10-01.** Illustrative and unexecuted. These templates contain no
invented clinical trial results, treatment recommendations, or fabricated
funding/registration records. Fill them only from a verified study report.

Resolve the article type and current limit using `medical_journal_styles.md`
under `references/`. Do not use one journal's headings for another by default.

## Four-part research pattern

```text
BACKGROUND
[Clinical question and evidence gap, without overstating current knowledge.]

METHODS
[Design, setting, eligibility, actual sample size, intervention/exposure,
comparator, prespecified primary outcome, follow-up, and analysis population.]

RESULTS
[Numbers enrolled and analyzed; primary-outcome counts/denominators by group;
effect estimate with uncertainty and time horizon; relevant harms; missing data.]

CONCLUSIONS
[Conclusion at the scope supported by the design and interval estimates.]
```

NEJM-style research often uses these headings, but its current article-type page
controls. For The Lancet's research pattern, use Findings and Interpretation in
place of Results and Conclusions and include Funding as required. The funding
statement and trial registry must be real records, not template defaults.

## JAMA original-data pattern

```text
IMPORTANCE
[Why the question matters.]
OBJECTIVE
[Specific objective and prespecified hypothesis, if applicable.]
DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS
[Design, dates, setting, selection, numbers, and follow-up.]
INTERVENTIONS or EXPOSURES
[Only the heading applicable to the actual design.]
MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES
[Prespecified outcome definitions and assessment times.]
RESULTS
[Actual participant characteristics, estimates, uncertainty, and relevant harms.]
CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE
[Supported interpretation and applicability.]
TRIAL REGISTRATION (when applicable)
[Registry and actual identifier.]
```

JAMA's current original-data abstract maximum is 350 words. Its restrictions on
AI assistance differ by article type; this research template is not a workaround
for its Opinion, Letter, or Online Comment policy.

## The BMJ research pattern

```text
OBJECTIVES
[Question.]
DESIGN
[Study design.]
SETTING
[Care setting, location, and period.]
PARTICIPANTS
[Eligibility, selection, and numbers entering/completing the study.]
INTERVENTIONS (if applicable)
[What was delivered, when, and for how long.]
MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES
[Prespecified outcomes and assessment times.]
RESULTS
[Group denominators, estimates, uncertainty, and harms where applicable.]
CONCLUSIONS
[Interpretation that does not go beyond the data.]
TRIAL REGISTRATION (if applicable)
[Actual registry record.]
```

The BMJ ordinarily requests 250–300 words, allowing up to 400 when needed for a
CONSORT/PRISMA abstract. “What is already known” and “What this study adds” are
separate summary boxes, not extra abstract headings.

## Numerical consistency checks

Compare every count, denominator, percentage, effect direction, unit, time point,
and confidence level with the underlying analysis. Do not infer equivalence or
safety from a non-significant result. Do not compute NNT from a hazard ratio.
Trial-flow exclusions and missing outcomes must match the analysis strategy.
